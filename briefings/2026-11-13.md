## 1. SLOVENIA

**Government moves to strip citizenship from 13 Russian nationals.**
The interior ministry has asked administrative units to open withdrawal proceedings against
13 of the 93 Russians granted Slovenian citizenship by extraordinary naturalisation since
2015 — specifically those with registered residence abroad. Withdrawal is not automatic;
each administrative unit must first establish legal grounds (living abroad, holding another
citizenship, and having harmed Slovenian interests).

> **Framing differs noticeably here.** 24ur ran "Ministry *proposes* withdrawal"; RTV SLO
> led with "*Proposal* for withdrawal … *who in fact live abroad*", foregrounding the
> qualifier that narrows the story; STA's English service was most active — "Naturalisation
> withdrawal procedures *launched*". So the same action reads as a proposal, a
> narrowly-scoped proposal, or an initiated proceeding depending on the outlet. Coverage
> also hooks on the fact that one of the 93, bowler Viktorija Krivolucka, is a friend of
> President Nataša Pirc Musar — she lives in Slovenia and is *not* among the 13.

**Local elections Sunday, 15 November.**
Voters pick mayors and municipal councillors across all municipalities — 614 mayoral
candidates in 212 municipalities. Any seat undecided in round one goes to a 29 November
runoff. Early voting ran 10–12 November in Ljubljana and Maribor. Slovenia's Catholic
bishops have publicly urged turnout.

> *Unresolved:* sources I could reach disagree on the status of an associated referendum on
> local voting rights for non-EU permanent residents — one RTV page dates it to 11 October
> (55.30% yes) and calls it consultative, while other coverage implies a vote alongside
> Sunday's elections. I could not settle this without reading the sites directly. Treat the
> referendum detail as unconfirmed.

**Cash-access bill approved.**
The government backed legislation obliging retailers to accept cash and banks to offer a
free basic account package. Banks responded that key questions remain open — RTV SLO gave
that pushback headline placement, which the other outlets' snippets did not.

**Flooding and fuel.**
Heavy rain pushed rivers up nationwide, with the Vipava, Drava and Mura overflowing and
strong bora forecast on the coast. Separately, petrol fell to €1.506/litre at midnight, and
parliament passed fuel-price relief for hauliers and farmers (48–2).

**Also moving:** a court in Celje opened a judicial investigation into long-serving judo
coach Marjan Fabjan over suspected sexual offences; the Court of Audit found the education
ministry ineffective at digitising schooling.

## 3. SCIENCE & HEALTH

**Confirmed / published**

**26 million brain cells mapped into a basal ganglia reference atlas.**
A global team profiled nearly 26 million cells to build a cross-species reference for work
on Parkinson's and addiction. Published resource, not a clinical claim.

**Waist circumference beats BMI for classifying obesity.**
A Nature-reported approach using waist measurement predicts health outcomes better than
height-and-weight alone.

**Thousands of new pharmacogenomic variants from All of Us.**
A Science Translational Medicine study of ~100,000 Americans identified thousands of new
genetic variants affecting drug response; the cohort's diversity is credited as the enabler.

**American Cancer Society revises prostate screening guidance.**
Now recommends biennial blood tests for average-risk men aged 50–69 — a guideline change,
so directly actionable.

**Earth's early crust, 3.95 billion years ago.**
Ancient northern Chinese rocks show surface-altered oceanic crust was buried past 50 km and
partly melted far earlier than assumed.

**Preliminary / early-stage — labelled as such**

- **Granzyme K and ageing (mice, preclinical).** Aged mice engineered to lack granzyme K
  showed better heart and liver function and less kidney damage. Animal model only.
- **Alzheimer's protein-maintenance system (mice).** A neuron-maintenance protein system is
  disrupted in mice with neurological disease. Early mechanism work.
- **DeepMind genome-mapping AI.** An AI model reportedly replicated a gene-regulation
  finding with no wet-lab work. Described as impressive by scientists, but computational
  replication is not experimental confirmation.

**Public health / policy**

- **DRC Ebola outbreak is now the second largest on record.** Phone-data tracing indicates
  the Bundibugyo strain circulated undetected for weeks; researchers attribute the miss to
  surveillance failures, testing gaps and funding cuts.
- **RSV vaccines and antibody shots are sharply cutting hospitalisations** (Nature).
- **US imposes new synthetic-DNA ordering rules** to deter bioterrorism.
- **Researchers sued to block the NSF** from dismantling its social science directorate.
- A *Science* investigation found consumer neurotech firms have amassed large brain-wave
  datasets with few limits on using them to train AI.
