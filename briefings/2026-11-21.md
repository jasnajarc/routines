## 1. SLOVENIA

**Petrol threatens to limit fuel sales and close stations — government and company blame each other** (20 Nov)
Petrol has invoked an internal protocol allowing it to cap fuel dispensing or shut individual unprofitable sites, blaming state price regulation for squeezed margins. No locations or timing have been named.
→ **Framing split, the sharpest of the day.** *RTV SLO* frames it causally and neutrally: Petrol announces possible limits "due to lower profit." *Delo* leads with the government going "sharply after Petrol" over supply disruptions, with Petrol rejecting the accusations — i.e. a fight over who caused shortages. *Slovenske novice* (Delo's tabloid) escalates to "Shock for drivers! Petrol threatens…" plus a named culprit. *N1* goes practical-service: how much can you pump, and what will the PM say. Separately, a Brežice station closes 23 Nov–~4 Dec for tank repairs — unrelated maintenance that is easily conflated with the dispute. Note also that the 50 l/person cap and army fuel transport circulating in search results are from ~8 months ago (Iran war stockpiling), not current.

**2027 and 2028 budgets passed; spending tops €19bn for the first time** (20 Nov)
The National Assembly approved both budgets: 2027 expenditure €19.4bn (+6.9%) with a €2.3bn deficit; 2028 projects a deficit of 2% of GDP. The same session amended the regional development law.
→ Framing: RTV's headline emphasises the symbolic threshold ("spending above 19 billion for the first time"). Commentary flags that the 6 December referendum on the intervention law could knock ~€1bn out of the plan — a contingency the government's own framing downplays.

**Two Resnica MPs quit the party** (20 Nov)
Katja Kokot and Aleksandar Štorek are leaving Resnica, weakening Zoran Stevanović's position in parliament's leadership.
→ Framing: *24ur* sets Janša's prediction of "the implosion of a larger party" against the opposition's reading that the pair will now back the coalition — the same defection cast as either opposition momentum or coalition gain. Kokot's own line ("I don't want to wash dirty laundry in public") is given prominence, which keeps the cause unexplained.

**Janković under anti-corruption scrutiny over Podutik land** (20 Nov)
The anti-corruption commission is examining Ljubljana mayor Zoran Janković's land purchases for the planned Podutik Nordic centre. Separately, the Dars supervisory board was briefed on an interim report into suspicious transfers to fictitious foreign accounts.

**Also running:** RTV's draft 2027 programme plan omits Eurovision participation — TV Slovenija director Ksenija Horvat says there is no money; the Programme Council has not yet confirmed the plan, so it is not final (*Siol* frames this as a flat "we're not going," *RTV* as a draft still in process). Ljubljana was named European Capital of Tourism. Slovenia backs the European Commission's proposed minimum age of 15 for an independent social media account. The opposition is seeking constitutional review of the so-called Skok law; the minister says there is nothing contentious in it. Foreign Minister Tone Kajzer survived an interpellation on 17 Nov (47 against removal, 30 for) over Slovenia's block on an EU statement condemning Israeli settlement expansion.

## 2. GLOBAL

**First-ever Trump–Xi–Putin trilateral summit ends with a joint declaration** (19 Nov, Shenzhen)
The three leaders issued a declaration covering energy supply, international justice, UN reform and nuclear risk, and reaffirmed that nuclear war should never be fought.
→ **The widest framing gap in today's set.** *Reuters* calls it symbolic and stresses the absence of the arms-control talks Trump had sought, capping a three-day Asia trip with "few tangible victories." *AP* reports it more descriptively as a declaration calling for UN reform, increased oil production and continued nuclear-risk talks. *RFE/RL* counts four points and notes pointedly that **Ukraine is not mentioned at all**. *Al Jazeera* leads on the declaration's criticism of the International Criminal Court. Trump calls it "historic" and the talks "unbelievable." Outlets also disagree on basics — "peace declaration" vs "declaration on peace," three points vs four. No movement on Taiwan and no new nuclear agreement.

**ICJ rules Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya** (19 Nov, The Hague)
In the case brought by The Gambia, the Court held 9–6 that Myanmar's security forces acted with intent to destroy a substantial part of the Rohingya, and breached its duties to prevent and punish genocide. It did not find direct and public incitement by private actors.
Myanmar must legislate, investigate and prosecute, give assurances against repetition, and provide restitution including safe return of the displaced; compensation comes later and Myanmar reports back in two years. The ruling is final with no appeal, but enforcement runs through the UN Security Council, which has never enforced an ICJ ruling.
→ Framing: coverage divides between the historic-justice angle (*CFR*, *Middle East Eye*) and the read-across to Gaza — *Al Jazeera* ran two separate explainers on what it means for Gaza, while the *Jerusalem Post* frames the same ruling as something that "could undermine Israel's defense" in its own genocide case. Same judgment, opposite anxieties.

**Israeli tanks cross Gaza's "yellow line" in first ground operation since the truce** (18 Nov)
Tanks and bulldozers moved west of the ceasefire boundary near Nuseirat and Bureij in central Gaza, with evacuation orders and a road closure.
→ Framing and facts both contested. The IDF describes a targeted operation against a Hamas tunnel and infrastructure; COGAT said activity had ended and Salah al-Din road reopened by Thursday morning. Gaza's government media office puts displacement at ~6,000; Hamas calls it a flagrant violation of the deal. Defence Minister Katz says Israel is ready to take more land and make seized areas permanent security zones. **Sources disagree on whether it is over** — some report it concluded on 19 Nov, others describe continuing displacement. The Palestinian Health Ministry says more than 1,600 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire began.

**COP31 in Antalya ends without fossil fuel phase-out language** (19–20 Nov)
The final declaration text reportedly contains no mention of fossil fuels and no clear finance commitments from major emitters.
→ **Starkest single contrast of the day:** COP31 President Murat Kurum called it "the best COP ever"; Mohamed Adow of Power Shift Africa called it the worst climate conference he had attended in fifteen years. Climate Action Network International rejected the texts outright and urged Global South governments to do the same, accusing developed countries — the EU in particular — of blocking finance, just transition and adaptation. 350.org called the removal of transition language an "outrage"; Panama threatened to block any deal without an oil production cap. *Caveat:* the Heartland Institute's "exercise in futility" framing is from a climate-sceptic source and should not be read as part of the same critique. Reports were filed in the final hours, so the adopted text may differ.

**Ukraine: active shuttle diplomacy, contested specifics**
Witkoff and Kushner met Putin in Moscow on 12 Nov; a Berlin summit took place 19 Nov; Zelensky says the next round moves to a four-way format including Europeans. Meanwhile Russian strikes hit three Kyiv bridges, and Poland is investigating objects falling near the Ukrainian border.
→ **Verification flag:** the widely cited "28-point Witkoff plan" traces to late-2025 reporting, with a later 19-point framework and a Zelensky 20-point draft also in circulation. Sources carry inconsistent datelines and I could not confirm which document is the live text in November 2026. Treat any specific point-count claim with suspicion.

## 3. SCIENCE & HEALTH

### Confirmed / regulatory decisions

**FDA approves Bayer's Sunharbo to prevent stroke recurrence** (9 Nov) — first in a new class of clot-preventing drugs. Phase 3: secondary stroke in 6.2% on drug vs 8.4% on placebo, a 26% relative risk reduction. Note the absolute difference is ~2.2 points.

**FDA publishes its rejection letter for Summit's ivonescimab** (letter dated 13 Nov; STAT 18 Nov) — the agency found that 93% of progression-free survival events in the pivotal trial came from patients enrolled in China, with no overall survival benefit, and is demanding a new multiregional trial. A significant precedent for China-heavy trial packages.

**FDA grants GSK's Nelkarmi (neladalkib) accelerated approval** for ALK-positive non-small cell lung cancer — *accelerated* approval, so confirmatory trial data are still outstanding and clinical benefit is not yet verified.

**FDA approves Sanofi's Infilbray for type 3 Gaucher disease** — the first treatment for the neurological form, based on a late-stage study of just **43 patients**. Real approval, very small evidence base.

**Merck halts development of nemtabrutinib** after a review found benefits may not outweigh risks.

**FDA leadership in flux** — the White House pulled Heidi Overton's nomination for FDA commissioner on 17 Nov, leaving open questions about agency leadership and user-fee reauthorisation (STAT, 18 Nov).

**Antarctic ozone hole was the seventh largest on record in 2026** (NASA) — a completed measurement, not a projection.

### Early / preliminary — treat as provisional

- **Nature Aging (18 Nov):** cerebrospinal fluid flow during sleep is impaired in the aging human brain *before* any neurological disorder appears. Observational; mechanism and causal link to disease not established.
- **Nature (19 Nov issue, Vol 659 Iss 8141):** aged mice engineered to lack granzyme K show better heart and liver function and less kidney damage. **Mouse study** — no human evidence.
- **Nature Communications (20 Nov):** antihypertensive treatment more likely to prevent major cardiovascular events than to cause serious falls — a benefit-risk analysis, useful but not a trial result.
- **Stanford (via ScienceDaily):** patterns of brain activity said to predict Alzheimer's risk years before symptoms. Preliminary; predictive-biomarker claims of this kind have a poor replication record.
- **Forecast, not finding:** NASA scientists warn the intensifying 2026 El Niño could exceed 1997–98. A projection.
- **Operational, not research:** Voyager 1 has a computer fault blocking science data return; NASA says it has a plan.

→ Framing note: ScienceDaily and Live Science headline several of the preliminary items above in confident causal language ("can predict," "may have been born in") that the underlying papers do not support. The Nature and STAT originals are considerably more hedged.
