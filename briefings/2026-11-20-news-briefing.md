## 1. SLOVENIA

**Dars supervisory board backs management after multi-million-euro payment fraud**
The motorway operator sent money to fraudulent foreign accounts; chair Peter Drobež says Dars was the victim of an organised international criminal group, and the supervisory board expressed full confidence in management while ordering a forensic investigation. Infrastructure Minister Jernej Vrtovec has demanded that law enforcement and Dars establish immediately why the transfers happened.

> **Framing and fact disagreements — the sharpest of the day:**
> - **Amount:** 24ur and RTV report €1.8m across three transfers to an account in Germany (with cash withdrawn at Bulgarian ATMs). But a supervisory board report describes a separate case of **five payments totalling ~€2.5m** to an account presented as a new Strabag account, allegedly despite internal warnings. Sources do not agree whether €1.8m is the whole loss or only the first case.
> - **Resignation:** An RTV headline said the head of the finance department resigned; a later RTV piece says she reportedly did **not** resign after all.
> - **Tone:** 24ur leads with "supervisors defend management *despite* €2.5m fraud" and Žurnal24 with "full confidence"; RTV's later framing emphasises the board "does not rule out lawsuits against individuals." Same meeting, three different centres of gravity.

**Two MPs quit the Resni.ca parliamentary group and the party**
Katja Kokot and Aleksander Štorek left both the group and the party, citing disagreements that "have dragged on for more than a year"; Kokot declined to specify, saying dirty laundry shouldn't be washed in public. The group drops from five members to three — the bare minimum to exist — with Martina Haring taking over as leader.

> **Framing difference:** Government-side sources stress this "does not change the position of the governing coalition." Opposition commentary focuses on whether Speaker Zoran Stevanović still has the votes to keep his post — a question the coalition declined to address. Svoboda has separately demanded a fresh allocation of National Assembly committee leadership posts before the December session.

**Health minister blocks ZZZS's extraordinary contribution increase**
Minister Tadej Ostrc rejected the health insurance fund's proposal to raise the compulsory contribution from €39.36 to €48.60 a month from 1 March 2027 (an extra €9.24, of which €5.49 was the extraordinary component), saying "there will be no extraordinary increase" and that the system's problems can't be solved by repeatedly burdening people. He wants ZZZS to find internal reserves instead; the fund's assembly still discusses the proposal on 25 November.

> **Worth noting:** The rejection covers only the *extraordinary* rise. The regular annual wage-indexed adjustment still applies, so the contribution is expected to reach ~€43.11 in March regardless — a nuance some outlets led with and others buried. Siol gives the current figure as €39.96 against most outlets' €39.36.

**Ljubljana heads to a mayoral runoff: Janković vs. Mesec**
After two decades in office, incumbent Zoran Janković faces Left co-leader Luka Mesec in a second round, following nationwide local elections across all 212 municipalities. Pre-first-round polling had Janković ~11 points ahead; one runoff poll puts it at 35.2% to 33.4%, while prediction markets implied ~57% for Janković — a much wider spread than the poll suggests.

*Also:* a wet, cyclone-driven few days, with 20–40 mm of rain forecast nationwide and snow in places.

## 2. GLOBAL

**Trump–Xi–Putin trilateral in Shenzhen produces a "peace declaration" that doesn't mention Ukraine**
The first-ever trilateral (18 Nov) ended with a communiqué covering UN reform, fuel supplies and nuclear risk reduction, plus an energy component and criticism of the ICC — but no reference to the wars in Ukraine or Iran. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Ukraine was not "the central topic."

> **Framing split, and it's a big one:** Analytical outlets (CFR, Euronews) note the absence of concessions on Ukraine or European security and read European reaction as *relief*. Opinion-led coverage ran the opposite way — a European diplomat told CNN it was a "Putin dream" marking his rehabilitation, and Chancellor Merz dismissed it as a "photo session," warning that reviving the "Yalta format" won't bring peace. US senators of both parties called it a photo op, with Republicans specifically attacking the energy provisions. The wire-style reporting is notably drier than the "Yalta 2.0" framing used elsewhere.

**Ukraine peace track: Europe's seat at the table is disputed**
Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Ukrainian and European officials in Berlin on Thursday, focused on security guarantees and postwar economic recovery. Zelensky said Europe "will be on the negotiating team, not somewhere, not separately."

> **Direct factual contradiction:** US envoys said Russia had agreed to European participation; Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said no such agreement was reached and that Europe must first show "readiness to contribute" — Moscow has also asked Europe to halt military aid as a condition. Washington and Kyiv reportedly want Europe to name a single representative. Note that the widely cited "28-point plan" dates to November 2025, not now; current reporting doesn't establish its status.

**ICJ finds Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya**
In a judgment delivered at The Hague on 19 November, the court ruled 9–6 that Myanmar acted with genocidal intent in northern Rakhine state and breached the Genocide Convention — the first time the ICJ has held a state responsible for genocide. It ordered Myanmar to investigate and prosecute suspects and to enact implementing legislation; the case was filed by The Gambia in 2019.

> **Framing differences:** Al Jazeera centres what the ruling means for Rohingya return and accountability; the Jerusalem Post ran a parallel analysis on whether the reasoning undermines Israel's defence against Gaza genocide charges. Genocide scholars quoted across outlets agree it "will not immediately change the situation on the ground" — the ICJ has no enforcement power.

**Anthropic's IPO is the largest on record; AI stocks are cooling anyway**
Anthropic sold 980 million shares at $83, raising ~$81.3bn and surpassing SpaceX's $75bn record; shares began trading on Nasdaq as ANT and closed at $97.17, up 17%, for a fully diluted value of ~$1.8tn — roughly the world's seventh most valuable company. The debut landed while the Nasdaq Composite sits more than 10% below its record high of six weeks ago.

> **Numbers to watch:** CNBC's headline cited a 34% pop against a 17% close — the two figures measure different points in the session, and some outlets put the opening value near $2tn. Analysts are cautioning against chasing the stock.

**European Commission cyberattack: Politico and the Commission flatly disagree**
Politico, citing an EU official, reported that an AI-assisted attack on Commission staff systems may have compromised a large volume of personal data, with most of the attack believed contained. CERT-EU reportedly traced the intrusion to a freely available AI vulnerability-scanning tool identified as ARTEX; systems were disrupted for more than ten days after detection on 22 October.

> **Contradiction, with a likely explanation:** Commission spokesperson Thomas Regnier said "we have found no evidence of a data leak affecting Commission staff." One outlet cites a 92GB stolen-data figure — but CERT-EU's own blog describes a *separate* breach of ~91.7GB from a compromised AWS account, published by ShinyHunters on 28 March 2026. The near-identical size and data types suggest the two incidents are being conflated. Treat the 92GB figure as unverified.

*Also moving:* Somalia ordered the UAE's ambassador out within 72 hours; Cyclone Akvan made landfall on India's coast; Türkiye added its Kizilelma unmanned combat aircraft to active inventory.

## 3. SCIENCE & HEALTH

**[CONFIRMED SURVEILLANCE DATA] Measles resurgence costs the US, Mexico and Bolivia their elimination status**
PAHO reports 58,213 confirmed cases and 82 deaths in the Americas as of 13 November, against 12,806 in the same period of 2025 — a 4.7-fold increase across 17 countries. Guatemala (34,860), Mexico (13,796), the US (5,182), Peru (2,928) and Canada (1,126) lead; Canada lost elimination status a year earlier.

> Two caveats worth carrying: case reports actually *fell* 38% in the most recent two-week period, and there is no single global WHO figure for November in circulation — an 87,539-case global aggregate circulating online comes from a non-official aggregator and should not be quoted as WHO data.

**[CONFIRMED TRIAL RESULT — NEGATIVE] Novartis abandons its Lp(a) heart drug**
A trial found the once-touted potential blockbuster did not reduce heart attacks or cardiovascular deaths, and Novartis is largely walking away. A clean negative result on a widely watched cardiovascular target, reported around the AHA 2026 meeting.

**[REGULATORY WARNING — RISK NOT QUANTIFIED] FDA flags a rare eye condition with GLP-1 drugs**
The agency warned that GLP-1 receptor agonists carry a potential risk of a rare eye condition. This is a labelling-level safety signal, not an established incidence rate.

**[EARLY-STAGE / ANIMAL STUDY] Granzyme K and ageing**
In Nature's 19 November issue, aged mice engineered to lack the protein granzyme K showed improved heart and liver function and less kidney damage. Mouse genetics with no human evidence yet — interesting as a target, not as a therapy.

**[INVESTIGATION, NOT A FINDING] Neurotech firms hold tens of millions of hours of brainwave data**
A Science investigation found companies have amassed vast consumer EEG datasets from brain-wave headbands with few constraints on use. Reporting rather than a research result, but the most policy-relevant science item of the week.

*Also:* the US imposed new rules on ordering synthetic DNA to deter bioterrorism; Merck and Isomorphic Labs announced an oncology drug-discovery partnership (19 Nov — a deal, no clinical data); Nature carried a research highlight suggesting availability of knappable stone, not just fresh water, constrained early human settlement.
