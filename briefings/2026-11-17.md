## 1. SLOVENIA

**Local elections: Janković forced into a runoff for the first time in 20 years.**
In Sunday's first round (15 November) Zoran Janković took roughly 35% in Ljubljana against
Luka Mesec (Levica) on about 26%, sending the capital to a 29 November runoff — the first of
Janković's near-two-decade tenure. Runoffs are needed in 45 municipalities in all; Celje's
Kovač held his seat outright.
*Framing split:* N1 reports Janša saying SDS voters simply will not turn out for the Ljubljana
runoff — read by opposition-leaning outlets as conceding the race and by pro-government
outlets as denying Mesec a mandate. Treat percentages as provisional: counts were still being
finalised.

**Maribor is a genuinely open contest between Frangež and Greiner.**
With ~99% counted, Matevž Frangež (SD/Levica, 28.08%) and Bor Greiner (22.89%) advance to the
29 November runoff; Andrej Bauman finished third. The seat was open because incumbent
Aleksander Saša Arsenovič did not stand again. Ptuj also goes to a second round.

**First interpellation of the Janša government: Kajzer defends himself over Israel policy.**
Foreign Minister Tone Kajzer spent today in the National Assembly answering an interpellation
filed by Svoboda, SD and Levica, who argue he breached the Foreign Affairs Act by blocking a
joint EU statement on Israeli settlements in the West Bank in August. Roughly 14 hours of
debate were scheduled; the vote follows. He is expected to survive — the proposers are well
short of the 46 votes needed to dismiss him.
*Framing disagreement, the sharpest of the day:* Kajzer himself calls it "a storm in a glass of
water" (carried approvingly by Portal24/Siol); **nova24tv** headlines it "ideological
performance more important than the state of the country"; **N1** and **Svet24** treat it as a
substantive test of whether Slovenia's EU foreign-policy line was broken by one minister.
Analysts quoted by Zanima.me split between "diplomacy" and "political show."

**Budget and the intervention-law referendum both still live.**
Parliament's finance committee took up the 2027 budget on 14 November — a €2.3bn deficit,
2.9% of GDP — together with an implementation bill handing the government added powers over
budget management and borrowing; opposition amendments to the 2026 budget were voted down on
15 November. On the referendum, N1 reports the government "has two main opponents" going in and
that the result could land near 50–50. *Note:* I could not confirm the referendum date from any
indexed source today — the 12 November briefing had it as 6 December, and nothing today
contradicts or confirms that. Finance Minister Šircelj continues to blame "inherited financial
disarray"; the opposition and unions continue to call the package a transfer to the wealthy.

*Also:* the DZ's mandate-and-elections committee unanimously approved members of the
commission to investigate alleged misuse of SOVA and the police (the "Black Cube" affair),
requested by Resni.ca, SDS, NSi, SLS and Fokus — the plenary must still confirm it. And in
sport, **Slovenia won in Skopje 3:0** (Šturm 29', 91'; Zec 66') to finish second in Nations
League B1 on 11 points, securing a promotion playoff in March 2027 — their first ever win in
North Macedonia. RTV's live page showed 0:1, 0:2 and 0:3 snapshots at different points; 3:0 is
the final.

## 2. GLOBAL

**Trump, Xi and Putin hold a first-ever trilateral in Shenzhen tomorrow, with nuclear arms on
the agenda.** The White House has confirmed the meeting on the APEC sidelines; the Kremlin puts
it on 19 November, with APEC running 18–19 November. Trump wants arsenal reductions and intends
to raise the Iran war; officials say Ukraine is *not* expected to be discussed, and no agreement
is expected from a first meeting. The last US–Russia arms control treaty expired in February.
*Framing divergence is wide here.* Beijing's foreign ministry calls the summit of
"groundbreaking significance" while simultaneously saying it is "neither fair, reasonable nor
realistic" to expect China to join trilateral disarmament talks now — the two statements are
the story. **Breitbart** and **Daily Caller** frame it as historic deal-making;
a widely-syndicated analysis argues the reverse, that Putin and Xi have "caught Trump in a
nuclear trap" by drawing him into talks that constrain the US without binding China. Al Jazeera
and CBS stay closest to the procedural facts.

**Iran: US intelligence flags a possible "dirty bomb" or chemical plot in Washington — and the
White House denies any credible threat.** CNN, citing seven US officials, reported Iran is
weighing options to target senior Trump administration officials with a radioactive or chemical
device in DC, and that intercepted communications claimed bomb-making material was already
stored inside the US. Officials are explicitly described as divided on the intelligence's
credibility and on whether Iran has people in place; sources characterise it as Iran generating
options, not an imminent attack. The White House told Newsweek there are "no credible threats";
ODNI declined to comment.
*This is the day's clearest framing gap:* **Breitbart** and **JPost** lead on the plot itself;
**Newsweek** leads on the denial; one aggregator tracked 29 outlets framing the same CNN report
differently. Everything here rests on anonymous sourcing with no official confirmation.

**Hormuz: more tanker strikes, and oil falls anyway.** A tanker was hit by an unknown projectile
while outbound, causing an engine-room fire (per UK maritime monitoring); the IRGC separately
claims two tankers struck mines and six caught fire over two days — claims that remain
unverified. A third US carrier, the USS Theodore Roosevelt, reached the region Monday per
CENTCOM. Brent nonetheless slid to roughly $92 (from ~$94 Monday), analysts attributing it to an
expected ~1.7m bpd surplus and recovering Gulf exports rather than to the attacks. Brent was
~$72 before the war began in late February; OPEC+ decides on 29 November.
*Sources genuinely conflict on the strait's status:* one tracker calls it effectively closed to
commercial shipping (3 transits on 8 November vs ~85/day normally), while other reporting says
flows have largely recovered to pre-war levels. I could not reconcile these.

**Ukraine hits the Kerch Bridge in what may be its largest drone attack of the war.** Russia
says it downed 1,336 drones in a single overnight wave; traffic on the Crimean bridge was
suspended early Monday 16 November with around 2,000 vehicles stranded. Ukraine's armed forces
published video of a Flamingo cruise missile striking the bridge.
*Damage claims diverge sharply:* **Maritime Executive** reports the navigation span was
destroyed, with debris falling into the strait's only shipping channel; **Kyiv Post** and others
describe a hit on one arch corroborated by satellite imagery; pro-war Russian Telegram channels
claim the structure shifted ~15 degrees, which nobody has verified. Crimea's Aksyonov confirmed
an attack but gave no damage detail. Timing matters — this landed two days before the Shenzhen
trilateral.

**Nvidia: Huang is gone, and reports Q3 today without him.** The board accepted his resignation
as president, CEO and director on 11 November, announced 12 November, effective immediately.
CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO; Harvey Jones, on the board since 1993, becomes its
first-ever chair; Wachtell Lipton is investigating the foreign-intelligence allegations. Shares
fell 4.34% on the announcement. Today's Q3 FY2027 report is the first without him.
*Unconfirmed:* Latin Times and FT report a push to reinstate him, with the board publicly
silent — that is reporting on a lobbying effort, not on a board decision, and should not be
read as a pending return.

*Also:* the FAA's 13 November nationwide ground stop — triggered by a communications failure
originating in the Chicago area that spread across the National Airspace System — caused
6,400+ cancellations and 8,000+ delays on Saturday alone and ran into a third day, with
11,500+ US flights cancelled or delayed on 15 November; the FAA said operations were stable
and normalising by late Sunday. Japan and South Korea scrambled fighters after a Russian
airspace incursion.

## 3. SCIENCE & HEALTH

**CONFIRMED — Real-world data show RSV vaccines and antibodies cutting infections and
hospitalisations substantially.** A Nature news analysis (12 November) pulls together national
rollout data, led by Denmark, which began vaccinating pregnant people nationwide on 1 October
2025; a Danish team under Johannesen found infant hospital admissions fell sharply. Supporting
figures from earlier work: ~81% reduction in infant RSV hospitalisation when maternal vaccine is
given ≥2 weeks before birth (England), ~68% effectiveness in infants under three months
(Pennsylvania), and ~60% effectiveness against documented infection plus >80% against
RSV hospitalisation for a single mRNA-1345 dose in adults 50+ (veterans cohort, Medscape,
4 November). This is accumulated real-world evidence across multiple countries — the firmest
item in this section.

**EARLY-STAGE TRIAL, label accordingly — adjuvanted RSV pre-F vaccine in transplant
recipients.** A phase 2b randomised trial of an AS01E-adjuvanted RSV pre-F protein vaccine in
lung or kidney transplant recipients published in Nature Communications on 13 November.
Phase 2b, immunocompromised population, small — not a basis for practice change.

**CONFIRMED (litigation filed, not decided) — researchers sue to stop NSF dismantling its
social science directorate.** FABBS, SESP, SANS and two Massachusetts researchers filed in
federal court in Massachusetts on 9 November to block closure of the Social, Behavioral and
Economic Sciences directorate, alleging violations of the Administrative Procedure Act and
NSF's enabling statute. SBE distributes ~$247m/year — close to two-thirds of all federal
grants in these fields. The complaint says NSF has already stopped SBE funding opportunities,
reassigned nearly all its staff and cut ties with collaborating scientists. NSF's spokesperson
says the directorate "has not been shut down." Plaintiffs warn it closes when the funding
extension lapses on 11 December absent congressional action; the January 2026 appropriation's
accompanying report had directed NSF not to cut SBE by more than 5%.

**The DRC Ebola outbreak is now the second-largest on record and the most geographically
widespread.** CDC figures as of 10 November: 11,145 confirmed cases, 5,437 deaths, with 83 new
confirmed cases logged on 11 November across three provinces and Ituri the epicentre. Nigeria,
Kenya and Uganda have all confirmed cases. ABC and the Washington Times describe the response
as out of control six months in. *Figures differ by cutoff* — WHO's 3 November count was 10,668
cases and 5,198 deaths, so CDC/WHO discrepancies are timing, not dispute.

*Also, pending:* the Senate HELP committee votes tomorrow (18 November) on Heidi Overton's FDA
commissioner nomination. Chair Bill Cassidy remains publicly undecided — he previously called
her role in changing vaccine policy "almost disqualifying" — and Republicans hold a one-vote
committee margin, so a single defection sinks it. STAT also reports conflicts of interest among
doctors on the Kennedy preventive-care panel.
