## 1. SLOVENIA

**Local elections tomorrow — 212 municipalities, and Ljubljana is the race with the sharpest framing split.**
Slovenia's ninth local elections since independence are Sunday 15 November, polls 07:00–19:00, with 1,605,948 eligible voters as of the 7 September call; runoffs where no candidate clears 50% are expected on 29 November. In Ljubljana, Zoran Janković leads a very crowded field (Luka Mesec/Levica, Klemen Boštjančič/Svoboda, Marko Koprivc/SD, Anton Rop, Aleš Primc, Aleksandar Repić and others).
*Framing disagreement:* Janković has said publicly he will win in the first round, but **Siol** ran the story as "Janković is convinced of a first-round win — but the polls he pays for say otherwise," reporting that the pollster gave his commissioned results to him alone. Published polls point to a runoff: Ninamedia/Dnevnik had him 33.2% to Mesec's 22.9%, another November poll 32.3% to 23.7%, and a head-to-head of decided voters 54.4%–45.6%. Polymarket prices him at ~67%. Worth remembering that in 2022 polling consistently overstated Janković's position relative to his actual result. **Delo** framed the whole cycle more neutrally as "old faces, new challengers."

**Maribor will get a new mayor whatever happens.**
Incumbent Saša Arsenovič is not running, leaving ten candidates. Večer's polling puts four in contention: Katja Koren (Demokrati/SDS/NSi/Fokus), Matevž Frangež (SD/Levica/cyclists' and pedestrians' list), Andrej Matvoz (Svoboda) and independent Bor Greiner, with Andrej Bauman running from Arsenovič's circle.

**Janša's cabinet posted a draft decree rewriting the National Security Council — then pulled it.**
The PM's cabinet published a proposed new SNAV decree among government materials on Thursday and removed it on Friday, calling it a working draft. The draft would have left it to the prime minister whether to invite the president, changed SNAV's composition and operational group, and converted SNAV into a state defence operational headquarters in wartime or emergency. Janša, speaking at an EU summit in Dublin, said the decree stays as it was in his previous term and that no decision had been adopted. President Pirc Musar's office called the withdrawal the right decision but said it "does not erase responsibility for its content," and said she expects to keep being invited.
*Framing disagreement — the clearest of the day:* **STA, 24ur and N1** report it flatly as a withdrawal plus the president's reaction. **Večer** goes much further in the headline — a draft that would have "squeezed the president, the parliament speaker and the opposition out of SNAV sessions" — and ran a companion piece asking whether this was "a mistake by Janša's cabinet or a political provocation." **Delo** led on the political reaction instead: Golob calling Janša "a coward." One Večer commentary claims the material was pulled specifically so Janša could tell Dublin no decision had been taken; that is an opinion piece's assertion with no independent confirmation.

**Football: Slovenia 0–0 Switzerland at a sold-out Ljudski vrt.**
Friday's Nations League group B1 match ended goalless. Switzerland dominated possession (over 70% in the first half) with seven shots, four on target, and Oblak well positioned; Slovenia's best chance was a Bijol header. The draw confirms Switzerland as group winners; Slovenia sit on seven points from four matches and go to Skopje on Monday to try to secure second. *Caveat:* Siol's headline says Slovenia "nicked a point but lost Jaka Bijol" — the coverage I could reach doesn't make clear whether that is injury or suspension.

*Also:* first snow of the season — snow down to roughly 500 m in places, about 25 cm lying at Kredarica on Friday morning, with rain in the lowlands. The intervention-law (ZIURS) referendum remains set for **6 December**; the 2027 and 2028 budgets were due at the finance committee 13–14 November but I could not confirm the outcome of those sittings from the search index.

## 2. GLOBAL

**Jensen Huang is out at Nvidia — resigned as CEO, president and director, effective immediately.**
Nvidia announced the transition on **12 November**: CFO Colette Kress becomes interim CEO and Harvey C. Jones becomes the company's first-ever board chair. It followed the board's 8 November disclosure that it was investigating reports Huang had been targeted by a foreign intelligence effort, and subsequent reporting of extramarital relationships including one with an alleged Chinese spy. Reuters reported the board met late Wednesday 11 November to decide; Bloomberg reported it had been working with an outside search firm.
*This overturns the 12 November briefing,* which recorded the removal as reported-but-unconfirmed. *Framing split:* **WaPo/Reuters/Bloomberg** framed it as a board weighing an ouster; **Nvidia's own release** is titled simply "NVIDIA Announces CEO Transition" and gives no reason. One detail to treat as unverified: Wikipedia's entry on the scandal names a different executive than the press reporting does, and I could not reconcile it against company statements.

**First nationwide FAA ground stop since January 2023 — and the official explanation is already contested.**
The FAA halted all US departures on Friday 13 November for roughly four hours, lifting it at 01:15 UTC on 14 November, with more than 7,800 flights delayed (FlightAware). The FAA's own statement cites communication problems affecting several Air Route Traffic Control Centers, still under investigation. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed aging routers and switches overwhelmed by data and giving inconsistent readings; DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there is no sign of a cyberattack, that officials are examining incompatibility between old equipment and newly installed technology, and that it was unrelated to Thursday's Microsoft outage.
*Framing/verification:* **CNN** reports some controllers doubt Duffy's explanation, so the official account may change. Separately, the FAA had rolled out an AI-powered program called SMART that week; **the FAA has not linked it to the outage and the vendor denies responsibility** — treat any connection as unestablished.

**IRGC claims it destroyed a US radar in Bahrain; the US denies it and says it shot down two drones.**
Iran's Revolutionary Guard says it struck a US early-warning radar on Bahrain — which hosts the US Fifth Fleet headquarters — in retaliation for an air attack on a telecom tower at Sirik, Hormozgan. CENTCOM says it downed two IRGC attack drones on Friday with no injuries or damage. Neither Washington nor Bahrain has confirmed any strike, and there is no independent evidence of damage.
*This is the day's starkest framing divergence:* **Al Jazeera** — "US denies Iran's claim it hit two radar sites in Bahrain"; Iranian state outlet **Kayhan** — "U.S. Radar in Bahrain Destroyed in Precision Strike"; **Reuters** adds the sober frame that if accurate it would be Iran's first claimed strike on US military assets in a Gulf state since early September. Coverage of the Sirik tower attack itself is thin and unconfirmed. One headline notes diplomats scrambling to avert war.

**Ukraine: the fight is now over who sits at the table.**
**AFP** reports Europe and Russia sparred on Friday over European participation in future talks, with Washington insisting Kyiv's European backers be present. Kremlin spokesman Peskov's comments suggest Russia has not committed to that; Moscow says it wants a full peace settlement, not a ceasefire. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas: "First, we need a ceasefire," rejecting Russian demands to discuss Europe's broader security framework. This follows the Abu Dhabi round, where both sides reported "some progress" and agreed a large prisoner exchange but nothing on territory. Zelensky says he expects Putin to attend talks in Turkey — his expectation, not an agreed fact; Putin has shown no sign of moderating territorial demands.

**COP31 in Antalya: Guterres's last climate summit, and fossil fuels are the deadlock.**
On 13 November Guterres called for "immediate and drastic" emissions cuts — on the order of 6% a year for several years — calling overshoot "a recipe for catastrophe," and proposed a Climate Justice Compact. **Reuters** reports major fossil fuel producers and consumers resisting any deal specifying firm new targets or a shift away from oil, gas and coal. Australia, leading negotiations under a split presidency, is gathering views on a consensus political text; Türkiye is pushing global electrification as the route to cuts, which **AP** flags as itself a point of contention. Adaptation finance and the future of the UN's mitigation work programme are unresolved. Talks run another week.

*Also:* the Dutch coalition averted collapse on Friday after seven-plus hours of crisis talks over wealth taxes — the tax deal passed by a razor-thin margin with the VVD voting against its own government; Jetten says "the talks are not over yet," Wilders calls the coalition "effectively dead," and it holds only 66 of 150 seats. Chris Van Hollen announced on 12 November he will challenge Chuck Schumer for Senate Democratic leader (Merkley and Sen.-elect El-Sayed have endorsed him; Schumer says he already has the votes). A global Microsoft 365 outage on 12 November hit Outlook, Teams, Copilot, Azure, OneDrive and the Store — Outlook reports peaked near 130,000 — which Microsoft attributes to an issue introduced during an earlier network capacity upgrade, explicitly not cyber activity. AP reports the ICC has limited its Afghanistan investigation to Taliban crimes. Eight people, including two children, died in a New York building collapse; ten died in clashes over gold mining restrictions in north-east Guinea.

## 3. SCIENCE & HEALTH

**CONFIRMED (full peer-reviewed results) — pelacarsen cut Lp(a) by 78% and changed nothing clinically.**
The Phase 3 Lp(a)HORIZON trial (8,323 patients) was published in the *New England Journal of Medicine* and presented at the American Heart Association meeting on 7 November. Pelacarsen lowered lipoprotein(a) by 78.4% at 12 months, but major adverse cardiovascular events occurred in 12.3% of the pelacarsen arm versus 12.1% on placebo (HR 1.02, 95% CI 0.90–1.15). Novartis has halted the whole ASCVD programme including the managed access programme. *Interpretation is genuinely contested:* some argue the Lp(a) reduction was still insufficient, others that short-term lowering cannot replicate lifelong genetically low levels. Analysts read it as bad news for every Lp(a)-lowering drug; the NEJM authors leave more room. STAT ran a 13 November piece on what heart drug research does next.

**CONFIRMED (government data, not a study) — international student arrivals in the US have plunged.**
*Nature*, 13 November: incoming student-visa holders fell from roughly 387,000 in August 2024 to about 277,000 in August 2026 — around 28% — per National Travel and Tourism Office figures, with India and China accounting for most of the decline. Scientists quoted worry about US science, since international students earn a large share of US science and engineering PhDs. Corroborating but *not directly comparable* measures: SEVIS data for October 2026 show active international students down 7.1% to 1,192,572 (India −13.5% to 316,893, steepest at master's level; China −10.2% to 220,157), and IIE reported new international enrolment down 17%.

**CONFIRMED (regulatory) — FDA approved Bayer's Sunharbo to reduce stroke recurrence (9 November).**
In the Phase 3 trial, 6.2% of patients on Sunharbo had a secondary stroke versus 8.4% on placebo.

**PRELIMINARY / OBSERVATIONAL — two to label carefully.**
A study of roughly 750,000 pregnancies found no link between early exposure to semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy) and increased pregnancy risk — reassuring, but observational, so it shows no association rather than establishing safety. And *Nature* on 13 November reports a new atlas of the basal ganglia built from about 26 million brain cells, linked to a range of diseases; that is a foundational resource paper, not a clinical finding.

*Also, funding not findings:* HHS will direct up to $4.9 million into research on ibogaine, a psychedelic being studied for PTSD and addiction.
