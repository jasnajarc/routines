## 1. SLOVENIA

**Government installs acting police chief; opposition cries political interference**
The government named Simon Slokan acting director general of the police from 23 November, replacing Danijel Lorbek, for up to six months while four applicants compete for the permanent post. *Framing split:* RTV SLO and N1 ran it flat and procedural ("Slovenia has a new temporary police chief"); the conflict framing came via PM Janez Janša, who rejected opposition claims of political influence over the police and said the government is "undoing the consequences" of the previous administration's actions. The opposition reads the same appointment as capture of the police leadership. (18–19 Nov)

**JEK2: Levica and NGOs demand the US nuclear agreement be published**
Levica is pressing the government to release the draft intergovernmental agreement with the United States on the second Krško nuclear unit, publish a full financial analysis including cost-overrun scenarios, and allow a consultative referendum. NGOs say the draft would hand Westinghouse priority on technology selection; the ministry has refused public access to the document. *Framing split:* N1 leads on the secrecy angle ("Is JEK2 being decided behind the public's back?"), RTV SLO frames it through Asta Vrečko's quote that the project is "too big to leave Slovenia's fate to one government's arbitrary decision" and her €20bn cost figure. Treat the €20bn as a party estimate, not an official costing.

**DARS defrauded of €1.8m; finance chief departs**
Motorway operator DARS transferred roughly €1.8 million to a fictitious foreign bank account; police have secured almost €1.6 million of it, per chief supervisor Nina Marin on 24ur's evening programme. DARS and its head of financial services, Monika Pekošak, have agreed to terminate her employment. (18 Nov) Separately, prosecutors and police confirm a multi-week investigation in the Celje area into disputed multi-million-euro investments, with no details disclosed.

**No extraordinary health contribution rise; 6 Dec referendum narrows**
Health minister Ostrc rejected the health insurance fund's proposal for an extraordinary increase in the compulsory health contribution, with €135 million to be found in reserves instead. The government backed advisory referendums on debureaucratisation and on banning face coverings in public, but it now looks as though voters on 6 December will decide only on the intervention law. Also moving: a draft road transport amendment scrapping mandatory taximeters and allowing app-booked rides with a known maximum price; tax allowances and income tax brackets rise just over 6% for 2027; several mayoral races from the municipal elections head to a second round.

## 2. GLOBAL

**Xi, Trump and Putin hold a three-way meeting in Shenzhen — today's biggest story**
The first trilateral of its kind, on the margins of APEC, with nuclear disarmament and the Iran war on the agenda. A senior US official pre-emptively lowered expectations, calling it an "entry point" with no agreement expected; the Kremlin said it would run under 60 minutes. Ukraine has no representative present. *Framing split:* wire and live-blog coverage treats it as choreography over substance; CNN's analysis goes further and questions the premise outright ("Russia and China are not America's friends. Why does Trump think they are?"). (19 Nov)

**COP31 heads into its final day in Antalya with no deal**
The summit closes Friday 20 November; a revised "cover decision" text was still awaited Thursday. The first draft, 18 pages released Monday, exposed opposing positions on fossil fuels, finance, science and trade, and contained clauses some countries have already rejected. The Turkish presidency is pushing a target of 35% of global energy from electricity by 2035, up from about 23%. A dispute over IPCC report timing has hardened — Denmark's climate minister: "There is no compromise when it comes to science." The US sent no delegation again. *Framing split:* COP31 President Murat Kurum said Wednesday's ministerial "divan" session made "significant gains"; French minister Monique Barbut said it may not have advanced negotiations at all. Reuters-sourced coverage leads on division, the presidency on progress. Side outcome: 23 countries endorsed the Antalya Action Plan on Integrated Fire Management.

**Strait of Hormuz escalation: US strikes Qeshm Island, tankers hit**
US aircraft struck an IRGC missile launch site on Qeshm Island with precision munitions; a US official called it self-defence after Iranian attacks on US forces, and Iran's IRNA cited a regional official saying the strike caused no serious damage. Iran's Navy claimed Wednesday that three oil tankers caught fire after striking naval mines east of the strait — **unconfirmed**. UKMTO reported a separate tanker hit in its engine room by an unidentified projectile 6nm north of Khasab, Oman; ADNOC reported one crew member missing and 11 injured from a Tuesday attack. Iran has also fired on a US air base in Kuwait and sites in Bahrain, which says it intercepted them. The US has blockaded Iranian ports and is escorting civilian vessels. *Framing split:* attribution is genuinely contested — Iran blames mine damage on ships ignoring its warnings; several shipping incidents remain unattributed in wire copy.

**Ukraine: Abu Dhabi talks produced a prisoner swap and little else**
The first Russia–Ukraine talks in nine months ended with one concrete result — a large POW exchange, size unconfirmed — and no movement toward a settlement. Kyiv rejected giving up the whole of Donbas; Putin is still pressing demands Ukraine has refused (Donbas control, a smaller Ukrainian military, abandoning NATO ambitions). Envoy Jared Kushner has floated tying a deal to a broader European security framework offering Ukraine guarantees while addressing Russian concerns. Russia holds roughly a fifth of Ukraine, but its 2026 advance has slowed and been reversed in places. (Confirmed reporting to ~12 Nov; today's Shenzhen meeting is the live thread.)

**Post-midterm realignment and a snap Spanish election**
Democrats took both chambers of Congress on 4 November in what outside coverage called a "seismic shift" and a sharp rebuke to Trump; VP Vance said Republicans got "shellacked" and conceded "economic pain," citing gas prices. Trump has said the US–Israel war on Iran likely hurt his party. Netanyahu's coalition lost its Knesset majority in last week's Israeli elections, and Spain's Pedro Sánchez has called snap elections for 29 November.

## 3. SCIENCE & HEALTH

**CONFIRMED — US and Mexico lose measles elimination status**
PAHO announced on 17 November that measles is no longer eliminated in the United States, ending 26 years of that status; the Washington Post had broken the determination on 13 November from an anonymous source. The trigger is a single transmission chain sustained beyond 12 months. CDC had confirmed 5,412 US cases in 2026 as of 12 November — more than double all of 2025 — with six deaths reported. Mexico is also affected. Countries losing the status must submit a corrective action plan; HHS Secretary Kennedy said the department will work to regain it. *Note the date discrepancy* between the 13 Nov determination and the 17 Nov formal announcement across sources.

**TRIAL FAILURE — "light and sound" Alzheimer's device misses its primary endpoint**
Cognito's Spectris AD device, which uses flickering light and rhythmic clicks to drive 40Hz gamma activity, failed to slow cognitive decline. In 673 randomised patients using the active or sham device 60 minutes a day for 12 months, the active arm did not outperform sham. Results presented 16 November at a Boston conference; **not yet peer-reviewed**, to be submitted to NEJM. Cognito's CEO points to an apparent benefit in the mild-cognitive-impairment subgroup and plans an FDA application next year — standard caveats about post hoc subgroups apply. *Framing split, sharp:* Nature's headline is "flops in long-anticipated trial"; the Boston Globe's is that the device "may slow Alzheimer's symptoms in some patients." Same data, opposite impression.

**PRELIMINARY — hypothalamus atlas maps Alzheimer's vulnerability**
*Nature*, 18 November: the HypoAD atlas pairs high-resolution MRI from 458 people with single-nucleus RNA sequencing of 614,403 nuclei, reporting that metabolism- and circadian-regulating subregions — and their inhibitory neurons and microglia — are selectively vulnerable in ageing and Alzheimer's. Descriptive/associational; no therapeutic claim.

**PRELIMINARY — All of Us finds thousands of new pharmacogenomic variants**
*Science Translational Medicine*, 11 November (via STAT): a study of nearly 100,000 Americans identified thousands of previously unknown variants in genes governing drug response, in a cohort substantially more ancestrally diverse than earlier pharmacogene work. Variant discovery, not demonstrated clinical utility. Also this week: NIH is moving its last group of research chimpanzees to a sanctuary.
